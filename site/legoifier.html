First time? Calibrate before a big print

Print a small one first and make sure it fits

Every printer, filament and frame is a little different. Five minutes of test printing saves a plate that will not go in, or falls out.

1
Print the calibration strip

Five snap-off tabs, each with one stud, from 4.80 to 5.20 mm. The dots count the size: 1 dot is the smallest, 3 dots is your current setting. They sit on a thin handle beside each tab, below the face that touches the frame, so only the stud is tested.

2
Press each tab into your frame

Into a hole of the grid (or the underside of a LEGO brick). The right one goes in with a firm push and holds upside down, but comes out by hand.

3
Type in the winner

Each dot is 0.1 mm. Two dots = 0.1 mm smaller than now, four dots = 0.1 mm bigger. Put that number in Stud diameter.

4
Check with a 2 × 2 plate

A 16 × 16 mm plate with four studs: it tests the spacing too. If it fits, every plate you make with these settings will.

04 / Mount your board

The plate clicks into the grid. The board goes on top.

Use whatever is in the drawer. These are the three ways the first Pip's boards are held on.

Board has screw holesBolts as prongs

Push four bolts up through the plate from underneath, heads in the pockets. They stick up like prongs. Slide a spacer (or a stack of nuts, or a cut piece of straw) onto each one so the solder joints under the board have room, drop the board on, and finish with a nut. It is the same idea as the standoffs in a computer case.

In a hurry, or no holesHot glue

Turn off the bolt holes, print the plate, and glue the board straight onto it. Put the glue on the parts of the board with nothing underneath, or add a few blobs as feet if there are solder joints in the way. It holds well and peels off with a little rubbing alcohol.

OptionalSquishy TPU pads

If you are not in a rush, print small washers in TPU and put them between the plate and the board. They take up a little vibration and stop a bolt from cracking a board. Honestly, they do not add much: bolts and spacers are plenty.

Building it in Fusion 360 instead

Do exactly this in Fusion

1
Sketch your plate

Draw the rectangle at 23 × 18 mm and extrude it 3.00 mm.

2
Draw just one stud

On the plate's face: circle diameter 5.00 mm. Place its center 3.50 mm right, 5.00 mm down from the top-left corner.

3
Sketch → Rectangular Pattern

Select that circle. Choose the horizontal and vertical directions. Set Distribution = Extent. Enter the quantities and distances shown above; point the pattern into the plate.

4
Finish Sketch → Extrude

Click OK, finish the sketch, select all circular profiles, then Extrude 1.80 mm outward using Join to make raised studs.
